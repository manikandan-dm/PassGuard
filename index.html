EDUCATIONAL MODEL

Rates must be finite numbers from 0.001 to 10¹⁸ guesses/second. Changes apply when you select “Update assumptions” and invalidate an existing report.

These durations assume uniformly random passwords and exhaustive search, with N/2 expected attempts. Real attackers try common passwords, dictionaries, and patterns first. Rate limits, MFA, salts, hashing costs, hardware, and attacker knowledge all change the outcome. No attack is executed.

GOOD SECURITY STARTS WITH UNDERSTANDING

Security essentials.

Simple principles for a more resilient digital life.

Put it into practice
01 / LENGTH

Give guessing a longer job.

Length expands the theoretical search space exponentially. Aim for at least 16 randomly generated characters where supported, or a long passphrase made from independently selected random words.

A familiar quote or a repeated word is not a random passphrase. A large score is not proof of randomness.

02 / UNIQUENESS

One account. One password.

Reused passwords enable credential stuffing: attackers try leaked credentials on other services. Even a very strong password should never be reused across accounts.

Give your primary email and password manager special care. They are recovery gateways to other accounts.

03 / RANDOMNESS

Predictable is still predictable.

Capitalizing the first letter, swapping a letter for a familiar symbol, or adding a year is well known to attackers. Character variety cannot rescue an obvious base word.

Create a random alternative
04 / LAYERS

Do not stop at a password.

Enable multi-factor authentication. Prefer a passkey or security key where possible, and keep recovery codes somewhere safe. Never share verification codes with unsolicited callers.

MFA helps reduce account takeover risk; it does not make password reuse safe.

05 / PHISHING

Check the destination.

A convincing login screen can still be fraudulent. Check the domain, avoid unexpected sign-in links, and use a password manager's domain-aware autofill.

A secure-looking padlock only indicates an encrypted connection, not an honest site.

06 / RESPONSE

Act on exposure, not on fear.

If a password is exposed or reused, replace it on every affected account. Review active sessions, recovery settings, and MFA. A dataset count is not the number of your compromised accounts.

Understand the exposure check

A few useful distinctions

Is entropy the same as password strength?

No. The length × log₂(pool size) formula assumes independent, uniformly random character choices. Human patterns violate that assumption. PassGuard reports theoretical entropy separately from its pattern-aware educational score.

Why do the estimated crack times look so large?

Exhaustive search considers every combination, while real attackers prioritize likely guesses. A common word with a numeric suffix can be guessed early even when the theoretical search space is enormous. Hardware, hash design, rate limits, and MFA also matter.

Should I change a good password on a fixed schedule?

Prioritize changing passwords when they are exposed, reused, suspected compromised, or required by your organization. Choose a genuinely unique replacement rather than a predictable increment.

TRANSPARENCY, NOT PROMISES

Privacy & limitations.

Know exactly what runs here, what leaves your device, and what the results mean.

No account required

Your password is not our data.

Analysis, generation, local checks, estimates, and reports run in your browser. There is no application backend, analytics, tracking, database, or persistent password storage. Theme and scenario preferences are also memory-only.

How sensitive data is handled

  • Passwords and hashes never go to localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB, cookies, URLs, or logs.
  • Typing, analyzing, opening sections, and generating passwords never initiate external requests.
  • Breach checks require an explicit click, use HTTPS with response padding, omit credentials and referrers, and time out after 10 seconds.
  • Input changes invalidate assessments and cancel in-flight checks. Reset clears all sensitive UI and application state.
  • Reports exclude password strings and hashes. HTML output is escaped and generated locally.
  • There are no third-party scripts, fonts, CDNs, or runtime dependencies.

Important limitations

  • The score is a documented educational heuristic, not a guarantee or industry-certified measure.
  • The dictionary and local list are intentionally small and English-centric; sophisticated patterns can be missed.
  • Entropy and exhaustive-search time assume uniformly random characters. They can hugely overestimate human-created passwords.
  • Attack rates are hypothetical. Password hashing costs, hardware, salts, MFA, and attacker knowledge vary.
  • A negative breach result applies only to the checked dataset. A failed check is not a negative result.
  • Browser extensions, malware, screenshots, clipboard history, and an untrusted host can still expose credentials.
